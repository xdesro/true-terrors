We walk together in an autumnal spruce-fir forest on a crisp mountain morning. The peaks are yet enwreathed in fog, and the sunlight comes at an uncanny angle through the conifers and aspens. The air is cool and the understory is damp, all but silencing our footfall at the trailhead. As we make our way up the trail through the woods, the path forward becomes harder to find and follow. The underbrush overgrows, the canopy above weeps heavy yellow leaves and rusty needles, and the pines lift snide roots to catch unwary heels and ankles. 

We laugh off this encroachment, reassuring each other it'll clear up soon, opening to a wide open sky, a marshy meadow, a gossipy little snowmelt creek making its way with first-day-of-school nerves down to meet the Colorado River. But it doesn't clear up, in fact it grows darker as Douglas Firs grow taller, blocking out that October sunlight. The trees are unkempt, sick with dwarf mistletoe and pine beetle. They lean claustrophobically into each other, and choke the young trees below out of their sun and nutrients and water.

All at once we are plunged into darkness. That promised meadow unseen, and the trail lost in all directions, scattered by a lack of care and by relentless, blighted growth. 

§

Well, I reckon this is it for us. We don't write code anymore, freelance budgets are token spend, our email inboxes are stuffed with letters no one wrote, our bosses sneer demands to use the tech that usurps us or be fired. And the tech is effective, too — all of our last-season nervous laughter about spicy autocorrect echoes off austere walls as agents write more and better code each day. Craft, and the nobility of a day's work, once-proud heraldry against inconsiderate design and careless release cycles, are threadbare banners in winter winds.

Bright-eyed technologists, come for the promise of creative problem-solving and good salaries, now squint into the unforgiving white glare of a new day. We're not saving the world, we're surveilling it. We're training the machines that will come tomorrow to threaten our livelihoods. We forgive ourselves daily for transgressions committed obliquely against our neighbors — laboring daily under the maniacal feet of transphobic, racist billionaires, who use the profits we grow for them to subsidize the withering and delitigation of our human and civil rights.

All the infinite promise of technology is swallowed by the maw of capital's seething hate of the labor on which it was for so long so reluctantly dependent. 

§

And my optimism fares no better than my peers. The organized labor revolution never came — workers walk a razorwire between rising costs of living and companies with bottomless coffers for legal violence, and for most of us, the risk is too high to try. Protests against the ecological impacts, or the extractive nature of the tooling and the work, or the ultimate impact to labor, are made for individuals, instead of attacking root causes.

Corporations have long abandoned an interest in delivering good products, narcotized in dogged pursuit of profit and bottom lines. Venture capital props up parasitic mutants that extract subscription fees and dignity from increasingly far-reaching arms of society, and the beasts stand on entrapping user experiences and gluttonous data mining in some twisted hope they might some day be profitable, no matter the cost.

§

So what else is there? For most of us, it's resignation — a get the bag and go home mindset. *It's just a job*. It's self-indulgent of me to not propose another path, to not think critically and synthesize some miracle, but I'm weary too. I've got no solutions to propose to those who have lost their enthusiasm and creative inertia to white-collar erosion. It's somehow all connected and totally disconnected, and quite by design there's no time in the day to make any sense of the whole dizzying machine.

I have found I really enjoy reading books by revered thinkers and working on old motorcycles, and you might see if that works for you also.

§

The groundcover of the forest stays woefully moist in the eternal shade of rotten overgrowth, and healing wildfire never comes.
